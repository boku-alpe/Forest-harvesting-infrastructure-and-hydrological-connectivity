## Stage-2 screening proposal — Härkönen et al. (2023)

**Item:** `10-PZYR5N6S` — "Reviewing peatland forestry: Implications and mitigation measures for freshwater ecosystem browning", *Forest Ecology and Management*, review article.

### Gate-by-gate evaluation

**Gate 1 — Full text retrievable.** PDF available (17 pp., open access CC BY). ✓ pass.

**Gate 2 — Stage-1 inclusion confirmed by full text.** This is the decisive gate. The paper is a narrative review of peatland-forestry impacts on freshwater browning (DOC/Fe export). The infrastructure variable discussed is **drainage ditches and ditch network maintenance (DNM)** — peatland-forestry hydrotechnical infrastructure, not Block-B AquaSilva infrastructure (B1 roads, B2 skid/snig trails, B3 retention/mitigation structures). Hydrological connectivity is explicitly invoked as a conceptual mechanism — drainage "multiplies the size of the stream network … thus increasing the hydrological connectivity between the catchment area and surface waters" (Härkönen et al. 2023, 3) — and ditch density is treated as a predictor of DOC and water colour  (Härkönen et al. 2023, 4). However, connectivity is operationalized as a driver of **chemical export (OC, Fe, browning)**, not of runoff generation, peak flow, or flood-relevant flux. The review's outcome domain is water quality, not the flood-hydrological connectivity that anchors the AquaSilva corpus  (Härkönen et al. 2023, 1).

Two defensible exclusion-reason strings exist; the first failed criterion in sequence is the infrastructure mismatch:

- **Primary proposal (Gate 2):** `no Block-B infrastructure as variable` — drainage ditches fall outside the B1/B2/B3 scope defined in the protocol.
- **Alternative (also Gate 2), if the screener accepts drainage ditches as a borderline B3 case:** `connectivity not operationalized as outcome` — connectivity is mechanistic narrative, not a measured/modelled outcome variable.

Rationale for choosing the first: in the AquaSilva connectivity-anchored taxonomy, Block B is tied to forest-operational infrastructure (Wemple/Croke/Takken lineage). Extending B3 to peatland drainage ditches would shift the corpus toward a different infrastructure tradition (peatland hydrology) and risk Type I errors in synthesis — exactly the scenario the protocol warns against.

Gates 3–5 are not reached once Gate 2 fails (sequential rule).
