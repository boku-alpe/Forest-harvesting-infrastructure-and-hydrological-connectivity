## Stage-2 screening — Lange & Haensler (2012)

### Gate-by-gate appraisal

**Gate 1 — Full text retrievable.** PASS. The 8-page PDF (Journal of Hydrology 464–465, 157–164) is available and readable. (Lange and Haensler 2012, 1)

**Gate 2 — Stage-1 inclusion confirmed by full text.** BORDERLINE → recommend FAIL.

The study is framed primarily as a runoff-generation / drought-recovery investigation in a 1.53 km² forest headwater (Engebächle, Black Forest), using hydrograph separation by EC, stream temperature, soil moisture, and weekly nitrate/silica/¹⁸O tracers.  (Lange and Haensler 2012, 1) (Lange and Haensler 2012, 3)

Forest roads (Block B1) do appear as a quantified runoff source: a mapping campaign identified 2.5 km of forest roads "directly connected to the investigated channel reach", and a back-of-envelope calculation (width 2 m, runoff coefficient ≈ 50 %) attributed ~65 m³, i.e. ~30 % of the event water during Event 1, to road surfaces. Wemple & Jones (2003) is cited as the anchor for this argument.  (Lange and Haensler 2012, 6)

However, this treatment is a single-snapshot structural-connectivity estimate (one mapped length, one assumed runoff coefficient, one event) embedded as a discussion sidebar. There is no event-wise variation of a connectivity variable, no metric (e.g., IC, road–stream crossing density, connected length per sub-event), no comparison between connected vs. disconnected segments, and no outcome regressed against connectivity. The study's analytical outcomes (RP, RY, DFP, α, event-water %, Table 1) characterize total catchment response, not the contribution of road connectivity per se.  (Lange and Haensler 2012, 5)

Per the connectivity-anchored inclusion criteria (structural ≠ functional, connectivity must be operationalized as an outcome to enter the core corpus), this falls short.

→ Terminal tag: **`006-Exclude-Quality`**
→ Exclusion-reason note (verbatim): **`connectivity not operationalized as outcome`**

### Rationale for screener (not a tag)

The paper would be a valid sensitivity reference for "forest roads can dominate event water immediately after drought" but does not itself supply an extractable connectivity–outcome relationship. If the synthesis later adds a secondary corpus for *qualitative process evidence*, this record would be a candidate for that secondary list rather than the connectivity core corpus.
