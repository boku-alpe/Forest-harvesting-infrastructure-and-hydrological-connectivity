# Stage-2 gate assessment — Fu et al. (2025)

## Gate-by-gate evaluation

**Gate 1 — Full text retrievable: PASS.** The primary PDF (13 pp.) is accessible.

**Gate 2 — Stage-1 inclusion confirmed by full text: BORDERLINE → recommend FAIL.**
- Block-B infrastructure as variable: clearly satisfied. The road (specifically a newly exposed road-cut slope in a dolomite hillslope) is the central manipulated/observed feature, with 156 rainfall events monitored from 2019–2022 (Fu et al. 2025, 1).
- Connectivity operationalized as outcome: **not satisfied in the connectivity-anchored sense.** The road-cut slope is studied as a flow-path interceptor (surface runoff SR vs. soil-epikarst lateral flow SEF), which is mechanistically the same process described in the Wemple/Croke road-hydrology canon (road as a new flow path that captures subsurface lateral flow). However, the framing throughout is rainwater harvesting efficiency for RBWH systems, with runoff coefficients (SR 2.2 %, SEF 16.0 %, RCF 18.2 %) reported as collection-yield metrics rather than as hillslope–channel connectivity indices, IC-type metrics, or delivery-to-stream measures  (Fu et al. 2025, 1) (Fu et al. 2025, 3). The terms "connectivity", "flow-path linkage to channel network", or "sediment/water delivery to drainage" are not used; the dependent variable is partitioning of intercepted volume into a roadside cistern, not transfer along a hillslope–channel continuum.

→ Proposed terminal tag: **`006-Exclude-Quality`**
→ Proposed reason note (verbatim): **`connectivity not operationalized as outcome`**

(Note: Gates 3–5 are not evaluated because the first-failed-gate rule applies.)

## Caveat flagged for the human screener

The mechanism studied — road-cut interception of subsurface lateral flow on a hillslope — is *substantively* a forest/rural-road connectivity process (cf. Wemple 1996, 2001; Croke 2005; Dutton 2005, on roads as flow-path interceptors and extenders of the drainage network). The dataset (3 years, 156 events, quantified SEF vs. SR partitioning on a dolomite hillslope) would be a strong empirical reference point if the SLR's exclusion rule is read narrowly as "framing" rather than "mechanism". Two options the screener may want to weigh:

1. **Strict reading (recommended above):** exclude at Gate 2; the paper does not treat connectivity as an outcome variable.
2. **Permissive reading:** retain as Include-Final on the grounds that road interception of SEF *is* a functional-connectivity process, and re-tag the outcome as `004-FlowPathInitiation`. This would require a documented deviation from the connectivity-anchored screening rule in the PSALSAR Appraisal log to avoid downstream Type I-error inflation.

My recommendation is option 1 to preserve the corpus discipline; the paper can still be cited as supporting mechanistic evidence in the Synthesis without being part of the core coded corpus.
