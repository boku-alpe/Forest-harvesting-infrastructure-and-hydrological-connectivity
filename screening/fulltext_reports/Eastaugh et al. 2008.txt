## Stage 2 Screening Assessment: Eastaugh et al. (2008)

### Terminal Tag Recommendation
**`006-Include-Stage2` AND `006-Include-Final`**

This record passes all five gates. The paper is explicitly connectivity-anchored: it develops and applies a probabilistic "volume to breakthrough" model to quantify road-to-stream hydrologic connectivity, directly addressing the SLR's core inclusion criterion. The study operationalizes connectivity through a derived connectivity index (Equation 16) with confidence intervals, comparing five road network configurations to assess how decommissioning affects hydrologic connection to streams.

### Key Extractable Findings (for Synthesis Phase)

**Connectivity metric**: Connectivity Index (IC) expressed as "equivalent width of directly-connected road surface" (units: metres), with 90% confidence intervals calculated via propagated variance from the volume-to-breakthrough model.  (Eastaugh et al. 2008, 2443)

**Critical finding on decommissioning effectiveness**: The actual decommissioning "was unlikely to reduce runoff to the stream network" and resulted in "a net reduction in stream water quality" relative to the original road configuration. The combined old (decommissioned) and new roads showed *increased* connectivity (6964 ± 514 m²) versus the original road alone (4777 ± 700 m²) for a 2-hour, 1-in-5-year design storm (35.8 mm).  (Eastaugh et al. 2008, 2445)

**Rainfall-dependent behavior**: New sidecut road was less connected than the original valley-floor road under small rainfall events (≤20 mm), but *more* connected under larger events (≥60 mm) due to longer segments and greater total area.  (Eastaugh et al. 2008, 2446)

**Anchor author linkages**: The study explicitly extends Hairsine et al. (2002) and Croke et al. (1999, 2006), confirming this paper belongs to the established connectivity modeling lineage identified in the anchor author framework.  (Eastaugh et al. 2008, 2438)
