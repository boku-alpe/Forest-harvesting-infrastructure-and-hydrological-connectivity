## Stage-2 screening recommendation — Fu et al. (2010)

**Proposed terminal tag:** `006-Include-Stage2` AND `006-Include-Final`

### Gate-by-gate appraisal (R-item track, since this is a review)

**Gate 1 — Full text retrievable.** PDF is available and complete (14 pages). Pass.

**Gate 2 — Stage-1 inclusion confirmed by full text.** Block-B infrastructure (B1 unsealed/forest roads) is the explicit object of the review, not contextual mention (Fu et al. 2010, 2). Connectivity is operationalized in two ways consistent with the SLR scope: (i) a flow-pathway typology distinguishing direct road–stream crossings, fully gullied, partially gullied, and diffuse pathways with their relative delivery efficiency  (Fu et al. 2010, 4), and (ii) a road-segment-to-stream sediment delivery ratio $SDR_{R\text{-}S}$ used as a quantitative connectivity metric  (Fu et al. 2010, 5–6). Pass.

**Gate 3 — R-items.**
- **R1 peer-reviewed:** *Environmental Modelling & Software*, Elsevier, peer-reviewed review article  (Fu et al. 2010, 1). Pass.
- **R2 topical alignment:** Core scope is surface erosion and sediment **delivery** from unsealed roads to streams, with explicit treatment of road–stream linkage pathways and delivery ratios; cites anchor authors Croke (2005), Hairsine (2002), La Marche (2001), Wemple (2001)  (Fu et al. 2010, 3–4). Pass.
- **R3 extractable synthesis content:** Synthesizes seven road models against an explicit assessment framework, tabulates reported sediment yields across climates/scales, and identifies systematic gaps (subsurface flow interception, imbalance between erosion vs. delivery representation, lack of uncertainty analysis)  (Fu et al. 2010, 1) (Fu et al. 2010, 4–5). Pass.

**Gate 4 — Sufficient sample/scope.** Seven models, multi-region empirical base (US PNW, US Virgin Islands, SE Australia, Malaysia, Thailand)  (Fu et al. 2010, 4). Pass.

**Gate 5 — Effect direction extractable.** Directional statements are derivable: e.g. delivery ratio declines with road-to-stream distance (100 % / 35 % / 10 % at 0 / 1–100 / 101–200 ft)  (Fu et al. 2010, 6); road–stream crossings deliver effectively all eroded sediment  (Fu et al. 2010, 4); cutslope/contributing-area increases raise runoff and surface erosion  (Fu et al. 2010, 3). Pass.
