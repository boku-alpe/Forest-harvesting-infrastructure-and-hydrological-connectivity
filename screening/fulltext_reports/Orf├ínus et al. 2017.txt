# Stage-2 Gate Assessment: Orfánus et al. 2017

## Gate-by-gate evaluation

**Gate 1 — Full text retrievable.** Pass. The 15-page PDF is fully accessible.

**Gate 2 — Stage-1 inclusion confirmed.** Pass. Forest roads and logging lines are the explicit independent variable (density up to 10 km/km², ~25 % incised to less-permeable subsoil), and connectivity is operationalised as an outcome via integration of the road network into the catchment drainage network and via simulated subsurface→surface flow conversion at road incisions. (Orfánus et al. 2017, 1) (Orfánus et al. 2017, 6) (Orfánus et al. 2017, 9)

**Gate 3 — Q-items (primary study: modelling + field mapping).**
- Q1 context: Gidra catchment (32 km², >95 % forested, 850 mm MAP, Cambisols on Variscan/Mesozoic units), 7 June 2011 event (104 mm/3 h).  (Orfánus et al. 2017, 6–7)
- Q2 design: HYDRUS-2D vadose-zone modelling coupled with GRASS-GIS terrain/road analysis and field soil survey along five transects.  (Orfánus et al. 2017, 6) (Orfánus et al. 2017, 9)
- Q3 reproducibility: 10-step methodology, BCs, rain forcing, antecedent state (−100/−200 cm), and soil hydraulic parameters (θr, θs, α, n, Ks) are tabulated.  (Orfánus et al. 2017, 6) (Orfánus et al. 2017, 8–9)
- Q4 infrastructure: width 4 m, total length ~160 km, density up to 24 km/km² locally, ~25 % deepened into the C-horizon.  (Orfánus et al. 2017, 8) (Orfánus et al. 2017, 13)
- Q5 connectivity operationalised: ridge-top roads excluded from drainage network; integrated "final catchment drainage network density" combining natural channels and runoff-active roads (Fig. 7).  (Orfánus et al. 2017, 9)
- Q6 effect direction derivable: roads contribute 60,000–70,000 m³ (≈20 % of direct runoff) to the flood, split into ~54,000 m³ infiltration-excess on surfaces and 5,638–15,392 m³ from subsurface capture.  (Orfánus et al. 2017, 13)

All Q-items pass.

**Gate 4 — Sufficient sample/scope.** Pass. Single catchment, single event, but with field-based parameterisation, distributed GIS analysis, and multi-scenario simulations across slope length × inclination classes.

**Gate 5 — Effect direction extractable.** Pass. Direction is unambiguously positive (roads increase peak/direct runoff) with quantified ranges.  (Orfánus et al. 2017, 13–14)

## Notes for the human screener

The `004` family arguably warrants a multi-value assignment (`FlowPathInitiation` + `RunoffResponse`) because the paper's contribution sits between mechanism initiation and volumetric runoff accounting; if the scheme requires a single value, `004-FlowPathInitiation` is the more distinctive descriptor. Similarly, `010-A2-FlowPaths` is the primary Block-A anchor with `010-A3-Metrics` as a defensible secondary if multi-tagging is permitted within Block A. These remain suggestions pending screener confirmation.
