## Stage-2 gate assessment — Foster et al. (1997)

**Recommendation: `006-Exclude-Quality`**
**Exclusion-reason note (verbatim): `connectivity not operationalized as outcome`**

### Gate-by-gate reasoning

**Gate 1 — Full text retrievable.** PASS. PDF is image-only (OCR not available to Beaver), but all 13 pages render and are visually inspectable by the human screener. (Foster et al. 1997, 1)

**Gate 2 — Stage-1 inclusion confirmed by full text.** FAIL.

The paper does contain a Block-B-adjacent infrastructure element: forestry **drainage ditches** dug during ploughing prior to plantation, which "altered the soil hydrology, allowing direct and rapid transport of soil-derived water to the main stream channels."  (Foster et al. 1997, 3) Four first-order drainage ditches (SE1, SE3, Tan N, Tan S) are explicitly sampled alongside the second- and third-order streams to test scaling and felling effects.  (Foster et al. 1997, 3)

However, the operationalized outcome variable is **stream hydrochemistry** (pH, ANC, Al³⁺, base cations, conductivity) during storm events — not a connectivity metric (no IC, no SDR, no flow-path mapping, no sediment/runoff connectivity index, no structural/functional connectivity framing in the AquaSilva sense).  (Foster et al. 1997, 1) (Foster et al. 1997, 2) The ditch network is treated as a sampling stratum and an implicit flow-path mechanism, but connectivity itself is neither defined nor measured. This matches the methodological framing's exclusion criterion: pure runoff/biogeochemical-hydrology study without explicit connectivity operationalization.

Per the sequential-gate rule, Gate 2 is the first failure → stop here; do not evaluate Gates 3–5.
