### Content-coding suggestions

- **002:** `002-Mixed` — paper treats roads and skid/extraction trails jointly as the infrastructure variable: "unpaved roads and trails in these forests generate more intense runoff… they are chronic sources of eroded soil which can easily enter channels if road networks are interconnected" (p. 8); "Sediment pathways in a tropical forest: effects of logging roads and skid trails" framing reiterated for SE Asia (pp. 12–13).
- **003:** `003-Review` — narrative review synthesizing examples from circum-Pacific temperate and SE-Asian tropical forests; no new empirical dataset of its own (structure of paper, pp. 1–14).
- **004:** `004-A1-ConnectivityConcepts` AND `004-A2-FlowPathDescriptors` — Type 1/Type 2 functional linkages (p. 7) are conceptual connectivity constructs; hillslope→hollow→channel→debris-flow pathways are flow-path descriptors (pp. 6–7, 11).
- **005:** `005-NorthAmerica` AND `005-Asia` — "Specific examples for temperate forests are drawn from the circum-Pacific region, particularly western North America and Japan" (p. 5); tropical examples "largely from humid tropical forests in Southeast Asia" (p. 10).
- **010:** `010-A1-ConnectivityConcepts` AND `010-A2-FlowPathDescriptors` — same evidence as 004; no quantitative connectivity index is computed, so `010-A3-ConnectivityMetrics` is NOT proposed.
- **020:** `020-B1-Roads` AND `020-B2-SkidSnigTrails` — roads as cutslope/fillslope destabilizers and runoff concentrators (p. 8, Fig. 4); skid trails and footpaths as connected sediment delivery networks in SE Asia (pp. 12–13, Fig. 5). `020-B3-Structures` NOT proposed — culverts/cross-drains/water bars are not analyzed as discrete variables.
- **030:** `030-empty` — paper does not analyze mitigation/BMP measures as a variable; riparian buffers are mentioned only briefly and noted to be ineffective in largest storms (p. 12), which is insufficient for a C-block assignment.
