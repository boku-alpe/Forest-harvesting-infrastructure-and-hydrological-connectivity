## Gate Report — Cienciala et al. 2021

**Study type:** Field (sediment budget / reservoir survey + repeat LiDAR), with qualitative-historical disturbance interpretation

**Gate 2:** FAIL — The study's primary outcome is specific clastic sediment yield, partitioning (bedload vs. suspended), and sediment sources; hillslope–channel connectivity is invoked only verbally as an interpretive frame ("Legacies of these disturbances appear to … limit … hillslope-channel connectivity"), and forest roads enter only as one of several sediment-budget inputs, not as a study variable whose effect on connectivity is tested. (Cienciala et al. 2021, 1) (Cienciala et al. 2021, 6)

**Gate 3:**
- Q1: PASS — Region (Selkirk Mts., NE Washington, inland PNW), catchment (Upper Sullivan Creek, 182 km² geomorphically connected), climate (mixed maritime/continental, ~1250–1400 mm a⁻¹, nival regime), and land use (coniferous forest, ~50–66% logged pre-2009, road network, Mill Pond dam) are all reported.  (Cienciala et al. 2021, 2–3)
- Q2: PASS — Classifiable as a field-based sediment budget / reservoir-sedimentation study with repeat-LiDAR DoD; not BA, CI, gradient, or paired-catchment in the connectivity sense.  (Cienciala et al. 2021, 3–6)
- Q3: PASS — Methods for reservoir bathymetry/sub-bottom profiling, coring, bulk density and LOI, trap-efficiency correction, partitioning thresholds, bank-erosion DoD, and source inventories are described in reproducible detail.  (Cienciala et al. 2021, 3–6)
- Q4: UNCLEAR — Roads (B1) appear as a sediment source with per-length erosion rates (paved Sullivan Lake Road 2 Mg km⁻¹ a⁻¹; undisturbed 3.8 Mg km⁻² a⁻¹; heavy-use logged 500 Mg km⁻² a⁻¹), but neither road density, total length, age, surface type, drainage design, nor spatial position relative to channels is characterized for the study basin itself — values are largely transferred from Reid & Dunne and Srivastava et al.  (Cienciala et al. 2021, 4) (Cienciala et al. 2021, 6)
- Q5: FAIL — Connectivity is not operationalized as a metric or index (no IC, no flow-path mapping, no mechanistic connectivity model); it is treated narratively, with structural disconnection only stated for Sullivan Lake as a sink.  (Cienciala et al. 2021, 1–2)
- Q6: UNCLEAR — A directional claim is made (disturbance legacy → reduced lateral activity and reduced hillslope–channel connectivity), but it is an interpretive inference, not a quantified effect on a connectivity variable.  (Cienciala et al. 2021, 1)

**Gate 4:** PASS — One complete iteration of the sediment-budget / partitioning / source analysis is reported for the basin over the 1910–2009 reservoir-infilling window, plus a 2012–2016 LiDAR DoD pass.  (Cienciala et al. 2021, 6)

**Gate 5:** FAIL — A clear effect direction on a connectivity outcome is not derivable; the study reports a sediment-yield value (~51 Mg km⁻² a⁻¹) and a qualitative narrative that disturbance legacies have *reduced* hillslope–channel connectivity, but without a quantified connectivity baseline or contrast this cannot be extracted as an effect estimate.  (Cienciala et al. 2021, 1)
