### Synthesis role

**Notes for synthesis phase:** Jones & Grant (1996) is a landmark paired-catchment study providing one of the longest quantitative records (up to 55 years) of road-driven peak flow amplification across a scale range of four orders of magnitude (0.6–600 km²). Its most distinctive contribution for AquaSilva is the mechanistic decomposition of road effects (flow routing / drainage network extension) from vegetation-removal effects (water balance), with the former dominating at large basin scales — a direct evidence anchor for the connectivity-peak flow linkage in RQ1 and RQ4. The explicit distinction between structural connectivity (road network topology, drainage density) and functional connectivity (realized hydrograph advance) maps onto the SLR's conceptual framing, even though the paper predates the formal connectivity vocabulary.
