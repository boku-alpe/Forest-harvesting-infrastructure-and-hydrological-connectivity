## Stage-2 Screening — López-Vicente et al. (2020)

### Gate evaluation

**Gate 1 — Full text retrievable.** PASS. Open-access PDF (CC BY) is in the library and fully readable. (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 1)

**Gate 2 — Stage-1 inclusion confirmed by full text.** PASS. Block-B infrastructure is treated as a variable, not as context: skid trails are explicitly enumerated per scenario (3 trails Pre-Fire/Fire, 4 trails Post-Fire) and parameterised in the LULC maps with their own C-RUSLE and Manning's n values, while check-dams are parameterised as effective sediment sinks (with height, width, storage volume and trap efficiency) and serve as the SC outlet target.  (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 6) (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 7) Sediment connectivity is operationalised as the primary outcome through four indices (IC-Borselli/B2019, IC-Cavalli/C2016, IC-Persichillo, AIC) yielding 66 SC maps at 2 m resolution.  (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 1) (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 4)

**Gate 3 — Q-items (primary study).** PASS on all six:
- Q1 (context): semi-arid Mediterranean, ~320 mm yr⁻¹, three headwater sub-catchments 66–194 ha, slopes 38–47%, Sierra de Los Donceles (SE Spain).  (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 2) (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 3)
- Q2 (design): GIS-based indices evaluation under a Pre-Fire / Fire / Post-Fire temporal design, validated against measured SSY at three check-dams — classifiable as Modeling with field validation.  (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 2) (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 6)
- Q3 (reproducibility): all four index equations given (Eq. 3–9), input layers (LiDAR DEM 2009/2016, SIOSE LULC, IGME permeability, Cenajo 5-min rainfall), software (SAGA 2.1.2, ArcGIS 10.3) and flow-routing choices (D-infinity, Planchon-Darboux) explicitly stated.  (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 4–5) (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 6)
- Q4 (infrastructure characterisation): check-dam geometry, trap efficiency and SSY are tabulated; skid trails are integrated via LULC, Manning's n and C-factor.  (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 6–7)
- Q5 (connectivity operationalisation): four IC variants plus two computation targets (SC_STREAM, SC_OUTLET).  (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 6)
- Q6 (effect direction): mean SC values per scenario × index × target are reported with Tukey significance letters.  (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 8)

**Gate 4 — Sample/scope.** PASS. Three sub-catchments × three scenarios × four indices × two targets (66 SC maps), with independent SSY validation at three check-dams over ~70 months.  (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 6) (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 7)

**Gate 5 — Effect direction extractable.** PASS. Quantitative directions reported: SC_STREAM increased on average +35 % (IC-B2019) and +42 % (AIC) in the Fire scenario relative to Pre-Fire, and partially reversed in the Post-Fire scenario (e.g. −11 % with IC-B2019; +5 % with AIC).  (López-Vicente et al. 2020, 8)

Note on AquaSilva relevance: the check-dam parameterisation (height, width, storage volume, trap efficiency) maps cleanly to Maßnahmenkatalog Type A (discrete storage), and the trail/Manning's-n treatment to Type B (area modifier) — useful as a parameterisation precedent for AP2, independent of the connectivity-focused inclusion decision.
