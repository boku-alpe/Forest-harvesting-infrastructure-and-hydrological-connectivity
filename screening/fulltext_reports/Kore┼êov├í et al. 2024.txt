## Stage 2 screening — Koreňová et al. (2024)

### Gate-by-gate appraisal

**Gate 1 (full text):** PDF retrievable, 20 pp., open-access CC-BY. Pass.

**Gate 2 (Stage-1 inclusion confirmed):** Block-B infrastructure (forest roads, paved and unpaved; bank fortifications; transverse in-channel structures) is treated as a variable affecting connectivity, not merely as context. The paper explicitly attributes lateral disconnectivity to the road network: "Lateral connectivity in Hučivá Desná and even more in Upper Moravice was significantly decreased by a high-density road network" (Koreňová et al. 2024, 12). Connectivity is operationalized as the outcome via three quantitative methods (ECA at six slope thresholds, IC-B with USLE C-factor, IC-C with roughness index)  (Koreňová et al. 2024, 8). Pass.

**Gate 3 (Q-items for primary study):**
- Q1 study context: Two forested headwater catchments in Hrubý Jeseník Mts., Czech Republic (Hučivá Desná 19.53 km², Upper Moravice 25.67 km²; middle-mountainous, schist/orthogneiss, coniferous commercial forest, 925–1168 mm yr⁻¹)  (Koreňová et al. 2024, 4). Pass.
- Q2 design classifiable: Hybrid field mapping + GIS-based modeling, with field survey used to validate ECA thresholds  (Koreňová et al. 2024, 5). Pass.
- Q3 method reproducible: DEM 5G (2.5 m), ArcHydro workflow, slope thresholds 2/5/8/10/15/20°, Connectivity Index Toolbox of Cavalli et al. (2013), 100-m segment classification protocol  (Koreňová et al. 2024, 8). Pass.
- Q4 infrastructure characterized: Road density and position relative to channel mapped; bank fortification length quantified (2.65 km / >30 % on Hučivá Desná; 0.89 km / ~1 % on Upper Moravice); 124 vs. 81 transverse structures mapped with type and functionality  (Koreňová et al. 2024, 9). Pass.
- Q5 connectivity operationalized: Both structural (ECA, IC) and partly functional (field-revised "functional ECAs") connectivity quantified  (Koreňová et al. 2024, 11). Pass.
- Q6 effect direction derivable: Parallel roads reduce slope–channel connectivity; effect stronger in Upper Moravice (revised ECA 2.14 km² vs. modelled ECA8 of 2.59 km², i.e. ~20 % overestimation without road correction)  (Koreňová et al. 2024, 12) (Koreňová et al. 2024, 15). Pass.

**Gate 4 (sample/scope):** Paired-catchment comparison, full longitudinal coverage of two streams (~16 km total), six slope-threshold scenarios. Sufficient. Pass.

**Gate 5 (effect direction extractable):** Negative effect of parallel roads on lateral connectivity is explicitly stated and quantified through ECA-vs.-field-mapping differences and IC-map comparison  (Koreňová et al. 2024, 15). Pass.

### Note for the AquaSilva corpus

This record is methodologically anchor-relevant for Block A3 (IC, ECA methodology in data-scarce forested headwaters) and Block B1 (roads as lateral disconnectivity agent), and supplies a transferable workflow for connectivity-anchored screening of small Wienerwald sub-catchments. The quantitative ECA-vs.-field-mapping calibration (8° threshold for Hučivá Desná, 10° for Upper Moravice) is a useful reference value for similar middle-mountain forested settings  (Koreňová et al. 2024, 15).
