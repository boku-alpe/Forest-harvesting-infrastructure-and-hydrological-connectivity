# Stage 2 screening — Leon et al. 2021 (Luquillo Experimental Forest)

## Gate assessment

**Gate 1 — Full text retrievable:** PASS. The 11-page MDPI open-access PDF is available and machine-readable. (Leon et al. 2021, 1)

**Gate 2 — Stage-1 inclusion confirmed by full text:** FAIL.

The paper is a *communication* on cartographic densification of the hydrographic network in the LEF using a new vertical datum (PRVD02) and 1 m² LiDAR-derived DEM. Its declared scope is improving map products for watershed/aquatic research, not quantifying how Block-B infrastructure (roads, skid trails, structures) modifies hydrological connectivity.  (Leon et al. 2021, 1)

Roads and culverts do appear, but exclusively as *cartographic constraints* on the GIS workflow — not as explanatory variables, treatments, or outcomes:
- The abstract frames roads/culverts as "constraining factors … that interrupt water flow and alter runoff pathways," i.e., features to be identified during mapping.  (Leon et al. 2021, 1)
- In the methods, bridges and unmapped culverts are listed as limitations of the flow-accumulation procedure that the analyst must work around manually.  (Leon et al. 2021, 6)
- In the results, anthropogenic channels/surfaces are described as requiring "significant investment of time and researcher validation to interpret and correct flow paths," again a workflow problem rather than an empirical analysis of infrastructure-driven flow rerouting.  (Leon et al. 2021, 8)

Connectivity is not operationalized as an outcome. The word "connectivity" appears once in the discussion, generically referring to ridge-to-reef ecological linkages and aquatic-fauna distributions enabled by future work — there is no structural- or functional-connectivity metric, no IC-type index, no event-based flux measurement, no road-to-stream linkage analysis.  (Leon et al. 2021, 9)

Because Gate 2 fails, downstream gates (3–5) are not evaluated (sequential rule).
