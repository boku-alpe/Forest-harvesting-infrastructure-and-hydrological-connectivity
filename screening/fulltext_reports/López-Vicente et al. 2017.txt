## Stage 2 Gate Evaluation — López-Vicente et al. (2017)

### Gate-by-gate assessment

**Gate 1 (Full text retrievable):** PASS. PDF available, 11 pages, complete.

**Gate 2 (Stage-1 inclusion confirmed):** PASS. Block-B infrastructure (forest roads, skidding trails, plus proposed check-dams) is the explicit treatment variable across six management-based scenarios, and connectivity is operationalized as the primary outcome via Borselli's IC index, updated with the D-infinity algorithm. (López-Vicente et al. 2017, 3)

**Gate 3 (Q-items, primary study — modelling with field validation):**
- Q1 (context reportable): PASS. Two paired subcatchments K2/K3 (33.2 ha) on Mt. Karasawa, Tochigi, Japan; mean slope 65–66 %; humid temperate, 1416 mm yr⁻¹; orthic brown Cambisol; *Chamaecyparis obtusa* / *Cryptomeria japonica* plantation.  (López-Vicente et al. 2017, 2)
- Q2 (design classifiable): PASS. Modelling study on a paired-catchment design with field validation from three hillslope plots and six gauging stations across a 40-month monitoring period.  (López-Vicente et al. 2017, 5) (López-Vicente et al. 2017, 8)
- Q3 (method reproducible): PASS. IC formula given explicitly (Eq. 1), D-infinity in SAGA 2.1.2, 0.5 m LiDAR DEMs from three airborne surveys, Planchon–Darboux pit-filling, all C-RUSLE weights for each land use tabulated with sources.  (López-Vicente et al. 2017, 3) (López-Vicente et al. 2017, 6)
- Q4 (infrastructure characterized): PASS. Forest road C-RUSLE = 0.5027, skidding trails = 0.2652; geometry, location and topological role described and mapped per scenario; check-dam siting criteria (>90th IC percentile, near trails, distributed) given.  (López-Vicente et al. 2017, 5) (López-Vicente et al. 2017, 6)
- Q5 (connectivity operationalized): PASS. IC reported at four scales (subcatchment / FMO-area / hillslope plots / streams) and per land-use class.  (López-Vicente et al. 2017, 8)
- Q6 (effect direction derivable): PASS. Quantitative deltas relative to pre-FMO baseline: +11.4 % (cypress, K2), +10.5 % (cedar, K2), +8.8 % (cedar, K3) in plantations; +8.6 % / +7.4 % in FMO-affected area of K2/K3; +5.4 % in K2 streams; skidding trails highest HC of all classes; check-dams reduce HC by −0.14 % (K2 streams) to −1.32 % (KS31 plot).  (López-Vicente et al. 2017, 6) (López-Vicente et al. 2017, 8)

**Gate 4 (sample/scope):** PASS. Six scenarios across 40 months; 189 storm events; six gauging stations; three time-matched LiDAR DEMs.

**Gate 5 (effect direction extractable):** PASS. Direction unambiguous — thinning + new skid trails *increase* HC; vegetation recovery and check-dams *decrease* HC.

### Brief methodological note for the screener

This record is methodologically central for the connectivity-anchored corpus: it pairs a Borselli-IC modelling design with paired-catchment field validation and treats roads, skid trails, *and* a retention structure (check-dam) inside one consistent metric. It is a strong anchor for the structural-connectivity side of the AquaSilva synthesis. The reported effect sizes are *structural* HC changes (IC-based), not event-flux measurements, so when used in Block C synthesis the values should be flagged as structural-connectivity deltas rather than functional retention coefficients.
