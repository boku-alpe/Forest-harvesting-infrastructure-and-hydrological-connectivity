### Recommendation

**Terminal tag:** `006-Exclude-Quality` *(borderline — flagged for human re-read)*
**Exclusion-reason string:** `connectivity only narrative, not operationalized`
**Confidence:** Low–Medium

**Rationale.** Methodologically the paper is strong and the substantive findings (rapid road-to-outlet flow routing, contrast with slow subsurface pathways in the unroaded basin) describe functional road–stream linkage in everything but name. However, by the PSALSAR connectivity-anchored design rule ("pure runoff-hydrology studies without explicit connectivity framing are excluded"), this paper sits on the runoff-hydrology side of the boundary: it never invokes connectivity concepts, defines no connectivity metric, and does not engage the Wemple/Croke/Takken/La Marche literature. It would be a textbook Type I admission if included on substantive grounds while ignoring the framing rule.

**Items flagged for human re-read:**
- **Gate 2 (b) / Q5** — screener should decide whether the quantified road→outlet routing (Tables III–IV, lag-time contrast) is sufficient to count as "mechanistic quantification of connectivity" under the protocol's operational definition, or whether the absence of connectivity vocabulary and anchor-author engagement is decisive.
- If the screener overrides toward Include, the paper would be a useful RQ5/RQ6 anchor for quantified road-segment contribution to peak flow under varying antecedent moisture (Japan / humid temperate / zero-order scale).
