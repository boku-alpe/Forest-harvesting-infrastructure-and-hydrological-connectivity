### Recommendation

**Terminal tag:** `006-Exclude-Quality`
**Exclusion-reason string:** `connectivity only narrative, not operationalized`
**Confidence:** High — the paper is a well-designed paired-catchment BACI study, but its outcome set is water-quality / hydrochemistry. The single qualitative reference to "hydrological flow paths" in the abstract is a mechanistic hypothesis for chemical shifts, not an operationalized connectivity variable. The authors themselves note ditching did not change the hydrograph pattern, which further weakens any connectivity-outcome reading.
**Items flagged for human re-read:** Gate 2(b) — screener should confirm that the flow-path language in the Discussion (pp. 9–15, not yet read) does not contain an operationalized connectivity treatment. If the Discussion stays at the level of the abstract's narrative ("changes in hydrological flow paths… most likely…"), exclusion stands. If a flow-path metric or quantified ditch-stream linkage is presented later, re-evaluate.

### Note for synthesis phase

Even though excluded from the connectivity-anchored core corpus, this study could be retained as **contextual background** for AquaSilva discussions of ditch networks as a Block-B infrastructure analogue: it provides quantitative field evidence (mid-western Finland, ~1.6–2.1 km² catchments, boreal climate, ~500 mm/yr precipitation, peat-over-till) that artificial drainage produces a >40× increase in suspended-material concentration and a ~1 pH-unit shift without changing the hydrograph — a useful counterpoint to studies that conflate ditching effects on water quality with effects on runoff connectivity.
