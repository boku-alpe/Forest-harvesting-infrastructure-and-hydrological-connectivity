# Stage 2 screening — Jing et al. (2022)

## Terminal-tag recommendation

**Proposed terminal tags:** `006-Include-Stage2` AND `006-Include-Final`

**Gate-by-gate assessment:**

- **Gate 1 (full text):** PASS. Complete 12-page article accessible. (Jing et al. 2022, 1)
- **Gate 2 (Stage-1 confirmed by full text):** PASS. Road networks are the explicit independent variable (Block B), and sediment connectivity (IC / modified ICR) is the operationalized outcome; the study objective explicitly couples the two.  (Jing et al. 2022, 3)
- **Gate 3 (Q-items, primary study):**
  - Q1 — context reported: Dongshuanghe watershed, 334.10 km², Dabie Mountain, monsoon climate, 1000–1200 mm annual rainfall, altimetric amplitude 744 m.  (Jing et al. 2022, 3)
  - Q2 — design classifiable as GIS-based modeling combined with structural network analysis, Random Forest and Geographical Detector Model.  (Jing et al. 2022, 5)
  - Q3 — methods reproducible: IC equations after Borselli et al. (2008), explicit ICR computation workflow (Road_Mask → FlowDir → DirMask → AccMask → DirFinal), RF parameters (n-trees = 1000, m-try = 2).  (Jing et al. 2022, 5)
  - Q4 — infrastructure characterized: four road grades (Rd1–Rd4) plus seven structural descriptors (density, slope, bending degree, α, β, γ, cost ratio).  (Jing et al. 2022, 4)
  - Q5 — connectivity operationalized as IC and a road-modified ICR in which roads act as permanent drainage lines / local sinks.  (Jing et al. 2022, 5)
  - Q6 — effect direction derivable: mean ICR (−0.97) > mean IC (−2.17); ICR declines quadratically with distance to nearest road (R² = 0.73); road structural variables outrank river variables.  (Jing et al. 2022, 1) (Jing et al. 2022, 7)
- **Gate 4 (sample/scope):** PASS. Watershed-scale analysis on 394 grid cells of 1 × 1 km.  (Jing et al. 2022, 4)
- **Gate 5 (effect direction extractable):** PASS. Quantitative, signed effects reported (e.g., 150 m threshold beyond which the road effect on ICR diminishes; Road_S explanatory power 0.127 in GDM).  (Jing et al. 2022, 7)

No exclusion-reason note required.

## Notes for the screener

The study is connectivity-anchored in the sense required by the SLR protocol: IC is the explicit outcome, and roads are introduced into the IC computation as drainage modifiers, not merely as a contextual landscape feature. This distinguishes it from runoff-hydrology studies that mention roads only descriptively. The methodological contribution (Road_Mask overlay on FlowDir to derive ICR) is a candidate anchor reference for the "structural connectivity under anthropogenic flow-path modification" cluster in the Synthesis stage, complementary to Croke (2005) for the field-process side.

One caveat for downstream coding: forested vs. agricultural land cover is not separated in the analysis, and "road" includes paved rural through national highways rather than forest roads in the Wemple/Croke sense. If the AquaSilva synthesis distinguishes forest-road connectivity from general rural-road connectivity, this paper should be coded as a transferable-methods reference rather than a direct forest-road case study.
