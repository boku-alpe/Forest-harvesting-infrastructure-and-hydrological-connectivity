### Recommendation

**Terminal tag:** `006-Exclude-Quality`
**Exclusion-reason string** (verbatim): `connectivity only narrative, not operationalized`
**Confidence:** High — the authors explicitly disclose that connectivity was identified conceptually but not quantified  (Ziegler et al. 2006, 1), and the §5.6 treatment remains qualitative  (Ziegler et al. 2006, 12).
**Items flagged for human re-read:**
- Gate 2(a) borderline: skid-trail and terrace Ks measurements (Table 5, p. 100) do constitute infrastructure-as-variable in a narrow sense; screener may wish to confirm exclusion is on Gate 2(b)/Q5 rather than Gate 2(a).
- Q4: infrastructure characterisation is partial (density only at catchment scale).
- Cross-reference: Sidle et al. 2004 (Hydrol. Process. 18, 703–720), cited repeatedly here, is the upstream paper that may carry the operationalised flow-path / sediment-pathway analysis — recommend prioritising that record for the corpus if not already screened.

**Note for screener:** This is a methodologically solid Ks/infiltration study that is connectivity-*adjacent* (anchor authors Sidle, Croke, Wemple are all cited) but does not itself operationalise connectivity. It would be a legitimate citation in the narrative framing of RQ1 (concept history) or as an effect-size source for road/skid-trail Ks in a runoff-hydrology corpus, but per the AquaSilva connectivity-anchored protocol it falls outside the core SLR. The upstream companion paper Sidle et al. 2004 ("Sediment pathways in a tropical forest: effects of logging roads and skid trails") is the more probable Include candidate from this research group.
