Systematic Literature Review Protocol (PSALSAR)
Version 3 – Revised
Forest Infrastructure and Precipitation-Runoff Connectivity

1. Objective and Guiding Principle
The objective is the systematic identification, appraisal, and synthesis of peer-reviewed studies examining the influence of forest infrastructure (roads, skid/snig trails, culverts, bridges, ditches, and retention/mitigation elements) on precipitation-runoff connectivity in forested catchments. The review analyses reported hydrological target variables framed within a connectivity context (including connectivity metrics and connectivity-related flow response parameters) as well as documented mitigation measures (retention/detention, infiltration/evaporation/disconnection, and road-drainage BMPs).
No supplementary search strategies (snowballing, citation tracking, hand searching) are employed. The literature search is conducted exclusively through the finalised search strings in the defined databases. If known, thematically relevant publications are not captured by the search strings, this is transparently documented as a limitation and justified in the discussion.
Scope limitation: Because the search architecture is anchored on connectivity terminology (Blocks A1-A3), studies that investigate forest infrastructure effects on runoff without using connectivity framing (e.g. studies reporting peak discharge without referencing hydrological connectivity) fall outside the retrieval scope. This is a deliberate scope decision to maintain thematic coherence and is acknowledged as a limitation.
2. Research Questions
RQ0: Main Question
How many scientific publications examine the influence of forest infrastructure on precipitation-runoff connectivity in forested catchments, and which hydrological target variables and mitigation measures are reported?
RQ1: Literature Characteristics
What are the temporal trends, geographic distribution, and publication outlets for studies examining forest infrastructure effects on precipitation-runoff connectivity?
•	RQ1a: How has publication volume changed over time (by decade/5-year periods)?
•	RQ1b: Which countries and regions are most represented?
•	RQ1c: Which journals, conferences, and publication types dominate?
•	RQ1d: What is the distribution of study types (empirical field, modelling, experimental, reviews)?
RQ2: Infrastructure Characteristics
Which types of forest infrastructure have been studied in the context of precipitation-runoff connectivity, and what are their reported characteristics?
•	RQ2a: What is the relative frequency of studies on roads, skid trails, and other linear features versus structural elements (culverts, bridges, ditches, cross-drains, water bars)?
•	RQ2b: What infrastructure characteristics are reported (density, age, design, maintenance status, spatial configuration, network topology, connectivity to stream network)?
RQ3: Hydrological Target Variables
Which hydrological response variables and connectivity metrics are measured or modelled in studies of forest infrastructure impacts on precipitation-runoff connectivity?
•	RQ3a: Which connectivity indices or metrics are used (structural vs. functional connectivity)?
•	RQ3b: What additional hydrological variables are reported alongside connectivity (runoff coefficient, time-to-peak, peak flow/HQ parameters, hydrograph shape, baseflow)?
RQ4: Study Design and Methods
What research designs, measurement approaches, and analytical methods are employed to investigate infrastructure-connectivity relationships?
•	RQ4a: What is the distribution of study designs (before-after, control-impact, gradient, modelling)?
•	RQ4b: Which measurement techniques and temporal scales are used (stream gauging, tracer studies, remote sensing, field surveys; event-based, seasonal, annual)?
RQ5: Reported Impacts and Findings
What are the reported effects of forest infrastructure on precipitation-runoff connectivity and associated hydrological response variables?
•	RQ5a: What is the direction and magnitude of reported impacts on connectivity metrics and flow pathway characteristics?
•	RQ5b: What factors moderate infrastructure impacts (catchment characteristics, storm characteristics, infrastructure design)?
•	RQ5c: Are there consistent patterns across infrastructure types, regions, or study designs?
RQ6: Mitigation Measures
Which mitigation measures are reported, and what evidence exists for their effectiveness in reducing infrastructure impacts on runoff connectivity?
•	RQ6a: What types of mitigation measures are described (cross-drains, water bars, retention ponds, road decommissioning, infiltration measures, buffer strips)?
•	RQ6b: What is the reported effectiveness of different mitigation approaches, and what design specifications or implementation guidelines are provided?
Summary: Reduced from 6 RQs with 24 sub-questions to 6 RQs with 13 sub-questions. Every remaining sub-question is answerable from the connectivity-framed literature the search architecture retrieves.
3. PICOC (Scope)
•	P (Population): Forested catchments/watersheds/headwaters (global).
•	I (Intervention): Forest infrastructure (linear + structural): forest/logging/resource/access/rural/winter/low-volume/gravel/unpaved roads; skid/snig trails; culverts/bridges/ditches/cross-drains/water bars/ponds/retention measures.
•	C (Comparison): With/without infrastructure; before/after; variants of density/design/mitigation (where available).
•	O (Outcome): Hydrological connectivity (precipitation-runoff), flow pathways, connectivity metrics, and co-reported hydrological response variables (including but not limited to peak flow/HQ parameters).
•	C (Context): Peer-reviewed; Language: English; Time period: unrestricted (search date documented).

5. Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion
•	Peer-reviewed publications
•	Forest/catchment context with relevant infrastructure (Block B)
•	Connectivity-related outcomes: studies must frame results using connectivity concepts, flow-path descriptors, or connectivity metrics as defined in Block A
•	English language; full text available
Exclusion
•	Off-topic; no hydrological connectivity framing
•	Duplicates, editorials, book chapters without peer review
•	Studies reporting only peak flow or runoff without connectivity framing
Screening
•	Two-stage: Title/Abstract, then Full Text
•	Exclusion reasons documented at each stage
•	PRISMA Flow Diagram
Grey Literature Handling
Google Scholar results are subjected to the same peer-review inclusion criterion. Non-peer-reviewed items (theses, technical reports, government documents) retrieved via Google Scholar are excluded during title/abstract screening. Google Scholar serves solely as a supplementary search to capture peer-reviewed publications with non-standard indexing.
6. Data Extraction
Data extraction is structured around the research questions:
Category	Variables (maps to RQ)
Bibliographic	Author, year, journal, country, publication type (RQ1)
Study context	Region, catchment size, climate zone, land use (RQ1b, RQ5b)
Infrastructure	Type (B1/B2/B3), density, age, design, maintenance, spatial config (RQ2)
Study design	Design type, measurement techniques, temporal + spatial scales (RQ4)
Connectivity outcomes	Metric(s) used, type (structural/functional), values, direction of effect (RQ3a, RQ5a)
Co-reported hydrology	Peak flow/HQ, runoff coefficient, time-to-peak, other variables (RQ3b)
Moderating factors	Catchment, storm, infrastructure design characteristics (RQ5b, RQ5c)
Mitigation	Type (C1/C2/C3), effectiveness, design specifications (RQ6)
7. Synthesis and Reporting
RQ1-RQ4 (Descriptive mapping): Evidence map with summary tables, geographic distribution map, temporal trend figures, and frequency matrices of infrastructure types, study designs, and connectivity metrics.
RQ5-RQ6 (Impact and mitigation synthesis): Narrative synthesis. Given expected heterogeneity in study designs, infrastructure types, geographic contexts, and outcome metrics, quantitative meta-analysis is not anticipated. Studies grouped by infrastructure type and connectivity metric for structured narrative comparison. Vote-counting (direction of effect) applied where sufficient studies report comparable outcomes.
Reporting: PRISMA-compliant including complete search log, PRISMA flow diagram, and inter-rater agreement statistics (if dual screening is applied).
8. Database Selection and Search Strategy
Databases
•	Primary: Scopus and Web of Science – comprehensive cross-publisher coverage, full Boolean syntax support including proximity operators and wildcards.
•	Supplementary: Google Scholar – captures potentially missed publications with non-standard indexing. Adapted strings using intitle: operator. Non-reproducibility documented as limitation.
Excluded Database
ScienceDirect was evaluated but excluded: (1) content already indexed within Scopus; (2) maximum 8 Boolean operators per query requires reducing strings to a degree that compromises precision and introduces systematic inconsistency between databases.
Search Logic
18 search strings defined per database (Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar):
Base queries (H01-H09): Ai + Bj + D
H01=A1+B1+D, H02=A1+B2+D, H03=A1+B3+D, H04=A2+B1+D, H05=A2+B2+D, H06=A2+B3+D, H07=A3+B1+D, H08=A3+B2+D, H09=A3+B3+D
Mitigation supplements (M01-M09): Ai + Bj + D + (C1 OR C2 OR C3)
M01=A1+B1+D+C, M02=A1+B2+D+C, M03=A1+B3+D+C, M04=A2+B1+D+C, M05=A2+B2+D+C, M06=A2+B3+D+C, M07=A3+B1+D+C, M08=A3+B2+D+C, M09=A3+B3+D+C
Complete Boolean strings documented in accompanying Excel workbook (SearchTracker sheet). Google Scholar uses adapted keyword density with intitle: operator.
9. 004 Hydrological focus (Outcome / dependent variable)
Definition: The hydrological or geomorphological variable on which the study measures, models, or interprets the effect of Block-B infrastructure. Outcome side of the causal chain; conceptual Block-A framing belongs in 010.
Permitted values (multi-value, max 3 tags per record, first = primary):
* 004-PeakFlow            : peak discharge magnitude, timing, hydrograph form
* 004-RunoffResponse      : runoff volume, coefficient, generation mechanism, streamflow thresholds
* 004-SedimentDelivery    : sediment yield, SDR, sediment-connectivity index (IC, ICR, READI) as Outcome
* 004-FlowPathInitiation  : new or reactivated flow paths, channel-network extension, gully formation, subsurface→surface conversion
* 004-empty               : none of the above applicable
Use rule: When a study reports a connectivity index (e.g. Borselli IC) as both methodological tool AND minimization target, dual-tag 010-A3 (concept/method) AND 004-SedimentDelivery (outcome). When a connectivity index is reported only as method without serving as the optimization target, tag only 010-A3.
